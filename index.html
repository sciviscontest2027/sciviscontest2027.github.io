2027 IEEE SciVis Contest

Micron-scale defects. Large-scale consequences.

Tiny defects hide inside 3D-printed metal lattices, and X-ray CT captures all of them, buried in gigabytes of volume data. This year's challenge: design the visualization that makes those flaws jump out, or build an autonomous AI agent that hunts them down and tells the story itself. Two tracks, one goal: show a materials scientist what went wrong, and where.

What is the SciVis Contest?

Welcome to the 2027 IEEE SciVis Contest, held annually as part of the IEEE VIS conference. The 2027 contest, created in collaboration with the Advanced Materials Process Science Group at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL), focuses on the visual analysis of X-ray computed tomography (CT) scans of metal lattice structures produced by laser powder-bed fusion. The data are nine 9 × 9 × 9 octet-truss lattices printed at LLNL with intentionally missing struts; like every printed part, they also contain unintended defects. A lattice relies on thousands of thin struts, which makes it unusually sensitive to such defects, and finding them is what this contest asks you to do.

The contest has two tasks at two scales of the same data (the individual strut and the entire part) and two tracks that differ in who creates the solution: a human visualization designer, or a fully autonomous agentic AI system.

DomainAdditive manufacturing, non-destructive evaluation (NDE), materials science
DataX-ray CT volumes, as-designed STL meshes, and a nominal node/strut graph for nine 9 × 9 × 9 octet lattices
TasksTask 1: Strut-level analysis  ·  Task 2: Part-level analysis
TracksVisualization Design Track  ·  Agentic AI Track
SubmissionReport (3–4 pages, VGTC format) + video (≤ 10 min); Agentic AI Track additionally submits a ZIP of defect CSV files
Prize$1,000 cash prize for the top entry, sponsored by ViSOAR

Two scales, one dataset

Task 1 looks at a single strut: is it as designed, and if not, what is wrong with it? Task 2 looks at the whole 20,000-strut part: where are the defects, do they cluster, and how do nine specimens compare?

Real parts, real consequences

The specimens are stainless-steel 316L octet lattices printed by laser powder-bed fusion with 0 %, 0.5 %, or 1 % of struts removed from the design (three specimens at each level; see the specimen table). The as-built parts also contain defects nobody planned.

Human or AI agent

Enter the Visualization Design Track with any tools you choose, or the Agentic AI Track, where an autonomous system must find the missing struts and build the visualization with no human intervention after launch.

Win a cash prize

Submissions are evaluated by the contest jury on criteria such as usability, creativity, and innovation. The top entry will be awarded a $1,000 cash prize, generously sponsored by ViSOAR.
